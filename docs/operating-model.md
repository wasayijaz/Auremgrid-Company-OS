# Agency operating model

Auremgrid Company OS is shaped around how a retainer studio runs: client trust,
cross-discipline delivery, review, and accountable follow-through.

## Four loops

- Money: the client sees a result and stays.
- Work: a request is captured, assigned, produced, reviewed, and shipped.
- Trust: a visible touchpoint keeps the account from going quiet.
- Learning: reusable rules are promoted into playbooks; client-specific rules
  stay in the client brain.

## Company structure

The operating structure has three complementary layers:

- Client success and account roles own relationship continuity, intake quality,
  status communication, scope clarity, and escalation.
- Discipline wings own production quality. Marketing, Design, and Paid Media
  each support executive-level production and lead-level review; Product,
  Video, and Operations follow the same producer-and-reviewer principle where
  applicable.
- Operations owns coordination across wings, evidence completeness, capacity
  checks, release logistics, and the visibility of stalled work.

Meeting capture is an intake responsibility, not a decision authority. It
records participants, source material, summaries, and proposed decisions or
actions. Client success or the accountable delivery owner confirms routing and
ownership before those proposals become commitments.

One person may cover several roles in a small team, but each workflow stage
should still name one accountable wing and role. A business title never grants
system access: permissions come from organization and workspace membership,
explicit capabilities, and approval policy.

## Front door

Work cannot start as an unowned message. Intake requires:

- what was asked;
- which workspace or account it belongs to; and
- who requested it and when it is needed.

Client success or the account coordinator confirms the request, Operations
checks scope and capacity when needed, and the receiving discipline accepts a
clear handoff contract before production begins.

## Forced finish line

A work item cannot enter review until its Definition of Done is complete. The
checklist varies by discipline but may include:

- responsive behavior checked;
- assets exported;
- creative kept inside platform safe zones;
- copy proofread;
- evidence attached; and
- handoff notes written.

The appropriate lead closes internal review. Abandoned review is a first-class
failure, not an invisible stall. Client or launch approval remains a separate
gate when the workflow requires it.

## Client brain

Each workspace has a client brain containing:

- snapshot;
- brand rules;
- landing-page rules;
- ads rules;
- design rules;
- email rules;
- dos and don'ts; and
- open loops.

The brain is how an agency starts work. The evidence layer underneath it is how
the agency proves a claim. Proposed meeting outputs and uncertain information
remain proposals until reviewed.

## Trust signal

Last touchpoint is treated as a leading retention signal. Client success owns
the cadence and follow-up loop; account leadership owns escalation when silence,
risk, scope, or decision delay becomes material.

## Level-based work routing

Task intent maps to capability tags and one of four levels:

- L0 Execute for bounded, repeatable production;
- L1 Operate for routine work requiring contextual judgment;
- L2 Build for implementation, diagnosis, and independent verification; and
- L3 Reason for strategic, ambiguous, or high-risk decisions.

The level applies to a task, not permanently to a person or job title. Higher
levels may handle lower-level work, but cost policy prefers the cheapest
qualified level. Eligibility also requires the correct workspace access,
tools, write policy, capacity, and workflow role. Level routing cannot bypass
permissions, evidence gates, or human approval.

## Implemented foundation and next layers

Today the system implements the canonical people and workspace boundaries,
client meetings and proposed outputs, signals, work lifecycle, cross-wing
workflow contracts, evidence and approval gates, handoff acknowledgements,
skills, availability, and person-level capacity snapshots. Business roles are
currently present primarily as person metadata and workflow role labels.

The next layers are explicit role and wing assignments, primary and backup
client-success ownership, role-aware stage assignment, meeting-capture
responsibility, and capacity derived from assigned work, leave, and time. Those
layers should extend the existing people, client operations, workflow, and
permission systems rather than create parallel authorities.

## Room to grow

External knowledge, search, communication, task, design, and campaign systems
remain adapters rather than sources of authority. Auremgrid remains canonical
for tenants, permissions, provenance, work state, approval state, and audit.
